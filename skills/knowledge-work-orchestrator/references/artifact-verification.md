# Artifact verification

## Research report

- Check source authority, dates, and direct support for material claims.
- Separate facts, calculations, inferences, and unresolved questions.
- Look for missing counterevidence and scope limitations.

## Decision memo

- Confirm the decision, decision owner, criteria, assumptions, and constraints.
- Compare only real alternatives.
- Include the strongest reason against the recommendation.
- Trace material numbers to supplied data or cited sources.

## Spreadsheet

- Inspect schema, units, date ranges, and definitions.
- Check formulas, references, errors, and calculated values.
- Preserve the source file; create a new reviewed artifact for material edits.
- Re-open the saved workbook and verify the result.

## Document

- Check audience, purpose, structure, claims, citations, and placeholders.
- Verify headings, tables, links, and final rendering when layout matters.
- Preserve the author's intended voice when editing supplied prose.

## Presentation

- Confirm one narrative and one job per slide.
- Check hierarchy, legibility, evidence, speaker context, and visual consistency.
- Render every slide and inspect the final output.

## Public content

- Verify factual claims, names, links, calls to action, and platform requirements.
- Confirm the content preserves the intended voice.
- A ready draft is not published evidence.

## Operational artifact

- Confirm owner, input, output, state, deadline, dependencies, and completion evidence.
- Separate local preparation from external execution.
